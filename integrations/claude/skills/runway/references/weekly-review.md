# Weekly review — and the reset

The weekly review is what makes the rest of GTD work: once a week the user looks at
everything, so that for the other six days they can trust the lists and stop carrying
things in their head. Your role: prepare, ask, write down. The user thinks and decides.

Scale it to the system. With fewer than about thirty open tasks this takes twenty to
thirty minutes. Keep all three phases even when it is short — the phases are the point.

If the repository has its own weekly routine, that routine leads; contribute the runway
steps below where it calls for them.

## Prepare (you, silently)

Summary (if available), inbox, next, waiting, someday, projects, tickler (if available),
tasks completed in the last seven days (only if the server can filter by completion date —
do not pull the whole history).

## Get clear

1. **Mind sweep.** You only ask trigger questions; the content is the user's. A few per
   round: open promises? things you are waiting on that nobody
   tracks? upcoming events, trips, deadlines? money, contracts, renewals? people you owe a
   reply? things that nag you? Every answer goes straight into the inbox, untouched.
2. **Inbox to zero** with `references/clarify.md`.

## Get current

3. **Next actions.** Go through the list: tick off what is done, capture follow-ups,
   rephrase anything that has been sitting because it is not really a next action.
4. **Calendar, two weeks back**: anything that needs follow-up? **Two weeks ahead**:
   anything to prepare? (You ask; the user looks.) Results become tasks.
5. **Waiting for — the complete list.** For each: still waiting? follow up now (you draft,
   the user sends)? give up? Move `scheduled` forward.
6. **Projects, one by one.** Is the outcome still wanted? Is there a `next` action? Is it
   really active, or on hold? For every stalled project: define the next action now, or
   plan it (`references/planning.md`), or put it on hold, or close it.
7. **Date hygiene.** Overdue items: done, new honest date, or no date. Question every
   `due` that is not a hard deadline.
8. **In no list.** Stand-alone tasks without a status tag: `next`, `someday`, or gone.
9. **Areas.** Does every area of responsibility have at least one project or action — or
   is it deliberately quiet? (Monthly is enough; ask when it has been a month.)

## Get creative

10. **Someday/maybe.** Activate, keep, or let go. Letting go is a success, not a failure.
11. **New ideas.** "Is there anything you would like to start?" Capture, do not plan.

## Close

Record the review (server timestamp if supported, otherwise the repository's own marker).
Finish with a short tally: inbox, projects reviewed, stalled fixed, waiting followed up,
someday activated or dropped — and the two or three things the user named as most
important for the coming week.

Higher horizons (goals, vision, purpose) are not part of the weekly review. When it has
been about a quarter, ask one question: "Do your projects still serve your goals?" and
leave it at that.

---

## Reset — after a gap

When the last review is more than ten days ago, do not start with the full weekly review
and do not comment on the gap. A system that greets the user with guilt gets closed.
Offer a fifteen-minute reset; numbers only, neutral tone:

1. **Inbox**: clarify what is quick, move the rest to one honest pass later.
2. **Overdue**: done, new honest date, or no date — in one shorthand round like the daily
   review.
3. **One `next` action per active project.** Projects nobody wants to think about go on
   hold.

Then record a review and suggest a fixed weekly slot in the calendar. A calendar
appointment is the most reliable reminder there is, and it needs no infrastructure.
